WORKSHOP 1 · ARTIFACT 1

Beyond the Chatbox — AI and ML Timelines

An interactive timeline exploring major and incremental developments in artificial intelligence after the launch of ChatGPT, from November 2022 through October 2026.

Introduction

For our Machine Learning Fundamentals workshop, my group created Beyond the Chatbox to turn our classroom discussion about AI developments into an interactive learning experience.

Description

The website presents selected milestones, explains why each development matters, and distinguishes major breakthroughs from smaller changes in everyday AI use. Visitors can explore events, filter by year, use a guided presentation, and answer a short quiz.

Objective

Our goal was to communicate the evolution of AI clearly to classmates and other learners, while encouraging readers to look beyond headlines and consider how changes in capability, access, and responsible use affect people.

Process

We discussed important AI developments in class, selected events for the timeline, organized the material into major and incremental milestones, drafted explanations, and built an interactive presentation. We explained how generative AI assisted the work.

Tools and Technologies Used

ChatGPT for research organization, event summaries, presentation notes, and website code; an image-generation tool for conceptual cover artwork; HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the interactive site.

Value Proposition — Unique Value

The timeline brings historical context, event explanations, a guided presentation, and a quiz together in one experience rather than offering a static list of dates.

Value Proposition — Relevance

This project helps me practice communicating technical change to a broad audience. My engineering background encourages me to distinguish what a technology can do from how reliably and responsibly it can be used.

WORKSHOP 2 · ARTIFACT 2

Adaptive Learning in Semiconductor Manufacturing

Leadership Discussion: Adaptive Learning and Change Management

This artifact connects adaptive learning and change leadership with my professional experience as a Failure Analysis Engineer in the semiconductor industry. It highlights how engineers and leaders can use feedback, failure data, teamwork, and continuous improvement to respond to changing technical conditions.

Objective

To demonstrate how adaptive learning supports successful change by helping people evaluate new information, adjust problem-solving methods, and improve processes as technology and workplace needs evolve.

Professional Experience

In my professional experience as a Failure Analysis Engineer in the semiconductor industry, I understand the importance of continuous learning and adaptation to change. My responsibilities include investigating hardware failures in PCBAs and server systems, analyzing test results, identifying root causes, and collaborating with engineering, quality, and production teams to implement solutions. Monitoring test failure trends, improving troubleshooting procedures, and implementing corrective actions can help prevent the same problems from happening again.

When a new product is introduced into production, unexpected hardware failures or testing issues may occur. Instead of following the same troubleshooting approach every time, engineers need to analyze new failure data, learn from previous issues, collect feedback from technicians, and adjust their methods. Updating troubleshooting guidelines and sharing technical knowledge with team members can improve problem-solving and production quality.

AI-Generated Change Scenario

The hypothetical scenario considers a manufacturing company introducing an AI and machine learning system to predict equipment failures and identify possible production quality issues. At first, employees may find it difficult to trust the system because they are more familiar with traditional testing and troubleshooting methods. The system may also produce inaccurate predictions during its early stages.

An adaptive leader would introduce the system gradually, provide employee training, collect feedback, and monitor its performance. The leader would work with the technical team to review results, improve the model, and make necessary changes to the implementation process. This approach gives employees time to understand the new technology while allowing the organization to learn from early results.

Leadership Takeaways

  • Use evidence: Review test results, failure trends, and feedback before deciding what needs to change.
  • Support people: Provide training and encourage employees to share concerns and observations.
  • Adjust the approach: Treat early problems as information that can guide improvements to the technology and the implementation plan.
  • Keep human judgment involved: Check AI predictions and technical recommendations instead of accepting them without verification.

Skills Demonstrated

Root-cause analysis Problem-solving Continuous improvement Cross-functional teamwork Adaptability Responsible AI awareness

What I Learned

This activity helped me understand that successful leadership is not only about introducing new technology. Leaders also need to understand how people respond to change, listen to feedback, and make adjustments based on actual results. In failure analysis, the same mindset is useful because each issue can provide information that improves future troubleshooting and production quality.

ADDITIONAL COMPLETED COURSEWORK

AI Lab — StudySprint Chatbot and AI Tool Evaluation

In a separate group AI Lab assignment, we developed StudySprint, a conversational study-planning prototype for college students using Mizou, with ChatGPT assistance. We also compared three language-model responses, explored a Study Buddy GPT, and used STORM for a research article.

Project Goal

Help students organize assignments and exam preparation around their deadlines, available hours, breaks, and changing circumstances.

Our Process

We planned the chatbot, configured its instructions and welcome message, tested scenarios, reviewed its responses, and revised its rules. We documented both successes and failures rather than assuming the prototype was reliable.

What Testing Showed

The chatbot asked useful clarifying questions and declined a request to write a graded paper. However, it repeatedly made scheduling and arithmetic errors, including planning work after a deadline. A correct schedule required detailed human feedback.

Tools and Technologies Used

Mizou, ChatGPT, Study Buddy GPT, and STORM. The report documents the scope and limitations of each activity.

What I Learned

AI tools can support planning and explanation, but confident answers still need verification. Checking each time block, total, and deadline was essential to judging the output.
